# Review 2026-10-22: plan, idea, UI, features, market fit

Asked for: a review with no fixed rule and no sacred cow, and an improvement
plan. Written on `task/a11y-and-room-rows` at 1896541, on top of main
aa82ba1 (0.34.0). Numbers are as of 2026-10-22 unless dated.

## Verdict

The product is better than its audience knows. The audience is seven people.

Floorplan Studio is the deepest floor-plan card for Home Assistant that I
can find: 2D, 2.5D and 3D from one layout, a real editor with undo, outline,
layers, floor alignment and a scene designer, a phone sheet that lists what
needs attention, state text that matches HA, validation that survives a
hostile file, accessibility that passed axe. Rivals have none of the last
four. Five weeks of work went into 40 releases in 16 days.

Meanwhile: 7 stars, 1 fork, no issue ever filed, 49 page views in 14 days,
one outside reply on the forum thread. NeonPlan 3D got 840 stars in 25 days
with less product. easy-floorplan has 637 and ships weekly to a crowd that
files feature requests. The gap is not features. It is that nobody has seen
this, and that what they can see (README images from before 0.29, a demo GIF
from 2026-10-02) undersells it.

The second finding is cost. 29,190 lines of source carry 74,353 lines of
tests, a 10 to 14 minute local suite, CI runs of 17 to 50 minutes, a card
file of 4,313 lines and a size cap we sit nine bytes under. Each release
costs an hour of machine time and an HA install. That tax is paid on every
change to a product with no users yet, and it is why "small" items take a
day.

So: stop building for two weeks, show the thing, then build what people ask
for. Details and the plan follow.

things and starting from areas. Everything else is visibility.

### Where we fit

Three positions exist: the hacker's SVG (ha-floorplan), the pretty 3D house
(NeonPlan, floor3d), and the quick editor (easy-floorplan, Houseplan). We
sit between the last two with one thing none of them has: the plan as a home
summary. The phone sheet says "2 alerts, 2 lights, 4 sensors" before you see
the plan. The floor row says what is on. Rooms report what is in them.
Nobody else answers "what is wrong in my house" from a floor plan.

That is the line to say first. "The floor plan that tells you what needs
attention" beats "another 3D house", and we have the 3D too.

## Idea

Worth continuing: a floor plan is the one dashboard a non-technical member of
the household reads without training, the category has steady demand (four
new entrants in 2026, 1,600 stars on a decade-old SVG tool), and the field
reports say ours already does the job in a real house with three floors.

Risk, stated plainly: two rivals ship weekly to communities that already
exist, and one monetises. We are late and invisible. Being the one that
works properly (survives updates, phone, accessibility, validation, state
text) is a slower win than being the one that looks neon, but it is the one
that lasts. It needs visibility, and it needs patience; it does not need
more features right now.

## UI

Looked at `shots/current/` (346 images, rendered today) and the three house
screenshots in `docs/img/`.

Good:

- The 3D blueprint shot is the best frame we own: lamps cast light on the
  floor, walls are cut, the alarm's red cone reads at once. It is not in the
  README. `docs/img/card-3d.png` is from before 0.29 and the README says so
  in the alt text.
- The phone sheet (`card-frame-phone-half-blueprint`) is clear: breadcrumb,
  search, "Turn off on this floor", Attention 2, Active 16. It is the
  strongest screen for a first-time viewer and it is not in the README
  either (`card-phone.png` is from 10-06).
- The light popup (brightness, colour temperature, colour, Turn off, More
  info, Details) is a complete control, not a shortcut to more-info.
- The light theme holds: purple ring on the living room, hall in red,
  glows read on grey.

Not good:

- The Studio at 380 px (`editor-handalign-panel-blueprint-380px`) stacks
  three rows of toolbar over a plan and a panel three screens long. The spec
  says editing is desktop; the UI should say so too (a one-line banner at
  under 700 px: "Studio works best on a desktop; the card is for the phone")
  instead of a layout that half works.
- First run. The card says "No plan saved yet. Open Floorplan Studio in the
  sidebar to draw one." (`src/card/load.ts:12`). The Studio then opens on a
  blank grid with an empty outline and a "Nothing selected. Click the plan to
  edit." panel. File › Load demo exists (`src/editor/toolbar.ts:275`) but
  sits inside a menu; nothing on the blank screen points to it, and there
  is no "start from your areas". A new user draws their first wall from the
  Help overlay or leaves. This is the five minutes that decide a star.
- The demo house. "Living, Kitchen, Hall, Reading corner, Garden pond" with
  one sofa and a desk looks like a test fixture, because it is one. Every
  rival's README shows a home. The three blurred house shots in `docs/img`
  (an exception recorded in DECISIONS on 2026-10-04) are the pictures that
  sell; they show the toolbar from before 0.29.
- 2D default: the blueprint theme on a dark card with orange rooms is
  handsome, but every device is a filled dot until you hover. The 3D view
  shows icons on the dots; 2D shows them only at higher `detail`. A first
  look at 2D does not say "light, speaker, motion" the way the 3D shot does.
  Consider icons on by default at the card's default size, dots only when
  the plan is small.
- Thirteen themes (`THEMES` in `src/core/render.ts:136`): blueprint,
  midnight, light, slate, terminal, solarized, ha, coffee, a-team, space,
  cyberpunk, carpenter-brut, beach-house. Each needs the computed-style
  contrast pairs finding 10 demands, and each theme page in the README is a
  screenshot to keep fresh. Five would do (blueprint, light, ha, midnight,
  and one playful); the rest can stay in code but leave the front of the
  README.

## Features and scope

What the last 16 days bought (Sprints 27 to 35): floors and alignment, 2.5D
and 3D polish, hand alignment, card layout and touch, 3D framing, extras in
two parts, the car with nine models. Sprint sizes in PLAN.md put the biggest
effort into the editor rework (1.6), the entity picker (10), "decent before
publishing" (7), 2.5D/3D polish (28) and the car (35).

The car is the clearest case of building for one user: nine models, charge
state, door state, home and away, 24 screenshot variants, for a type that
one easy-floorplan issue asks for. It is good work. It is also the kind of
item that, had the README been current and the forum post fresh, would have
come after a user asked.

Surface today: 36 card config keys, 33 device types, 16 furniture symbols,
8 room kinds, 6 wall kinds, 13 themes, 3 views, kiosk, heat map, camera
thumbs, favourites, saved views, scenes, automations from the plan. Each
surface is tested, which is the right way to carry it, but it means every new
key or type lands in a 4,313-line card file and a 10-minute suite.

Missing against demand: vacuum position (the one live-moving thing people
ask for), rooms from HA areas, translations, non-admin wall tablets following
a save (no `subscribe` in `websocket.py`; a tablet logged in as a non-admin
shows a stale plan until reload), `getEntitySuggestion` for the 2026.6
picker.

## Engineering

Keep: the review findings (21 of them in CLAUDE.md) are a real asset, the
Chromium computed-style pairs, the hit-test-the-top-element rule, the gzip
cap, the Opus review after each sprint. The suite catches what matters.

Pay down, bounded:

- `src/card/floorplan-studio-card.ts` 4,313 lines, `src/editor/editor-app.ts`
  3,262, `src/core/render.ts` 2,012. Split the card into plan, sheet, popup
  and 3D-host modules. No behaviour change, one PR, diff reviewed by file.
- CI: one Playwright job of 17 to 50 minutes. Shard three ways; local
  default to the project the change touches, full before a tag.
- Card at 124,671 of a 124,650 gz cap: the next feature either removes
  something or raises the cap with a reason. The cap is doing its job; the
  decision is whether 3D-related code paths that live in the card (the 3D
  host, orbit state) move into the 3D chunk.
- Release cadence: 40 tags in 16 days. One a week, with a changelog
  paragraph a user can read, costs less and reads as stable.

## Improvement plan

Ranked. A before B before C. No new features in A.

### A. Be seen (two weeks, no features)

1. README media from 0.34: the 3D blueprint shot as the hero, the phone sheet,
   the Studio, one house shot per theme that stays on the front page. A
   30-second screen recording: open Studio, draw a room, drop a light, tap it
   in the card, watch it in 3D. Replace `demo.gif`.
2. A real demo layout. Two floors, eight rooms, furniture in each, a garden,
   a car, a vacuum. It replaces `demo/layout.json` behind File › Load demo
   and is what every screenshot uses. The tests keep the current fixture
   under another name; they pin its coordinates.
3. Forum: edit the first post of t/1026512 with the new media and a
   feature list; one reply per release after that. One post on
   r/homeassistant with the video. Add the HACS default listing and the
   2026.6 picker when they land.
4. `getEntitySuggestion` on the `customCards` entry so the 2026.6 picker lists
   us under Community with a live preview.
5. Issue templates (bug, layout that fails validation, feature), a Discussions
   tab with a "show your plan" thread. Right now a user with a problem has
   nowhere obvious to go, and 0 issues reads as 0 users.
6. Release weekly. Tag on Fridays.

### B. The first five minutes (two to three weeks)

7. Studio first run offers three doors: "Load the demo", "Start from your
   Home Assistant areas" (one rectangle per area on a grid, the area's
   entities placed inside as icons, every edge a wall; the user then drags
   corners), "Blank". This is what Houseplan sells and what the Interactive
   Floorplan thread asked for.
8. Studio under 700 px: a banner, not a stacked layout.
9. Non-admin tablets follow saves: `floorplan_studio/subscribe` in
   `websocket.py`, the card re-reads on the event.
10. Translations for the card's own strings (sheet, popup, floor row, errors)
    keyed on `hass.language`, shipped for de, nl, fr, it, es. The Studio
    stays English for now; it is the admin's tool.
11. 2D icons on at the default card size.

### C. What people ask for (after A and B, one per release)

12. Vacuum: live position from the vacuum's map or a `device_tracker`, a
    path trail that fades. Same mechanism later gives a person on the plan.
13. Sun: `sun.sun` elevation and azimuth tint the side of the house facing
    it in 3D, windows glow by day. Cheap in three.js, strong in a screenshot.
14. History: a slider that replays the last 24 hours from the recorder
    (`history/history_during_period` over the plan's entities). Field report
    asked; nobody has it.

### D. Pay down (ongoing, one item per sprint, never a whole sprint)

15. Split the card file. Then editor-app.
16. Shard CI.
17. Freeze the surface: no new theme, no new car model, a new config key
    needs a removed one or a DECISIONS entry that says why.
18. A performance budget test: a 200-device, three-floor layout renders a
    state update under a fixed time in Chromium; 3D is checked by hand on
    the tablet once per release and the number goes in the changelog.

## What I would stop

- Themes beyond five on the README front page.
- Per-user features before the README is current (the car was one).
- Daily releases.
- Reading "0 issues" as "no bugs".

## Decisions for Diego

1. Positioning line: "the floor plan that tells you what needs attention",
   or "the floor plan with a real editor"? Both are true; one goes first in
   the README and the forum title.
2. A feature stop for the two weeks of phase A, yes or no.
3. Release cadence: weekly, or keep tagging per sprint.
4. Translations: yes, and which languages first.
5. Themes on the README front page: all 13 or five.
6. The demo layout: build a real one (two floors, furniture, garden, car,
   vacuum) or keep the fixture.
7. Open from before: card size cap (raise with reason, or move 3D host code
   into the chunk); S23.F5 heavier dash on a selected zone; S19.E5 camera cone
   clip; the four release-box ticks in PLAN (S26.26, S27.17); leftover
   worktrees and branches.
